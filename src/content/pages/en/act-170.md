Act 170 is a Vermont law passed in 2026. It began as House bill H.955 and the Governor signed it on June 18, 2026. Among other things, it requires every school district in Vermont to join a **merger committee** that studies whether to join with nearby districts.

This page covers the parts of the law that apply to this committee. The [full text of Act 170](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Docs/ACTS/ACT170/ACT170%20As%20Enacted.pdf) is on the Legislature's website. Section 13 is the part about merger committees.

## What the law asks of our three districts

Section 14 of the law suggests which districts should study together. It puts Burlington School District, South Burlington School District, and Winooski in **Group 18**. Facilitators had to set the groups by October 15, 2026. They confirmed this group on September 25, 2026.

By September 1, 2026, the Vermont Learning Collaborative had to hire the facilitators. By September 15, 2026, each school board had to name at least one of its own members to the committee. Only sitting school board members may serve. If a member leaves their board, the board names someone new. By October 15, 2026, each committee had to hold its first meeting.

The committee must study whether it would be "advisable" or "inadvisable" to form a **unified union school district**. That is one school district, with one school board, that runs the schools for all the towns in it. Today each of our three cities has its own district and its own board.

The committee does not decide whether to merge. It makes a recommendation. Voters decide.

<h2 id="report">What the committee's report must include</h2>

[Act 170, Sec. 13(b)(5)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Docs/ACTS/ACT170/ACT170%20As%20Enacted.pdf) says:

> On or before September 1, 2027, each merger committee shall complete its final report and transmit it, along with proposed articles of agreement, as applicable, to the school board of each school district that the report identifies as either "necessary" or "advisable" if the merger committee determined it was advisable to form a new unified union school district, or to the school board of each school district participating on the merger committee if the merger committee determined it was inadvisable to form a new unified union school district. The report shall be transmitted to the Secretary of Education and the State Board of Education at the same time. If a merger committee completes its work before September 1, 2027, the committee may transmit its report to the applicable school boards, the Secretary of Education, and the State Board of Education at any time the report is ready for review, subject to the provisions of subsections (c) and (d) of this section.

[Act 170, Sec. 13(b)(4)(D)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Docs/ACTS/ACT170/ACT170%20As%20Enacted.pdf) says:

> A merger committee formed pursuant to this section shall prepare a report with its final recommendations as to whether it is advisable or inadvisable to form a new unified union school district. In addition to the report requirements in [16 V.S.A. § 708(c)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/16/011/00708), the final report of each merger committee formed pursuant to this section shall include the following:
>
> (i) the names of the school districts participating in the merger committee;
>
> (ii) an analysis of the strengths and challenges of the current structures of all "necessary" and "advisable" school districts;
>
> (iii) the merger committee's final recommendation as to whether it is advisable or inadvisable to propose the formation of a new unified union school district;
>
> (iv) an analysis of how the final recommendation will enable the merger committee member school districts to, under the foundation formula, maximize operational efficiencies, promote transparency and accountability, and encourage and support local decisions and actions that provide equal opportunities for an excellent education, all at a cost that parents, voters, and taxpayers value; and
>
> (v) if the decision of the merger committee was not unanimous, an analysis of the minority view of the committee, including an analysis of how any school district participating in the merger committee but not recommended to be part of the new unified union school district will, under the foundation formula:
>
> (I) provide excellent educational opportunities that allow students to achieve or exceed the State's Education Quality Standards;
>
> (II) maximize operational efficiencies that allow the district to meet or exceed the State's District Quality Standards;
>
> (III) provide resident students with a genuine opportunity to participate fully and to benefit from career technical education; and
>
> (IV) provide special education services.

[Act 170, Sec. 13(b)(4)(F)(i)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Docs/ACTS/ACT170/ACT170%20As%20Enacted.pdf) says:

> Each merger committee formed pursuant to this section shall consult with area career technical education (CTE) directors and shall document such consultation and any recommendations made by a CTE director in the merger committee's final report issued pursuant to subdivision (D) of this subdivision (b)(4). The final report shall also include an analysis of how CTE access will be achieved for all students residing within the proposed new unified union school district.

[Act 170, Sec. 13(b)(4)(F)(ii)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Docs/ACTS/ACT170/ACT170%20As%20Enacted.pdf) says:

> If a merger committee's member school districts send their resident students to a regional career technical center school district (regional CTE school district) formed pursuant to 16 V.S.A. chapter 37, subchapter 5A, the final report shall include an analysis of whether the applicable regional CTE school district shall dissolve, and the CTE center operated by the regional CTE school district shall be operated by a new unified union school district formed pursuant to this section. The analysis shall include the position of the regional CTE school district.

[Act 170, Sec. 13(b)(4)(C)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Docs/ACTS/ACT170/ACT170%20As%20Enacted.pdf) says:

> In addition to the requirements of 16 V.S.A. chapter 11, subchapter 2, a merger committee shall also explore the advisability and feasibility of a contemplated new unified union school district providing for the education of its resident students through local elementary schools, central middle schools, and comprehensive, regional high schools that provide each student with universal access to career technical education.

[16 V.S.A. § 708(c)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/16/011/00708) says:

> If a study committee determines that it is advisable to propose formation of a union school district, then it shall prepare a report analyzing the strengths and challenges of the current structures of all "necessary" and "advisable" school districts and outlining the ways in which a union school district promotes the State policy set forth in [section 701](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/16/011/00701) of this chapter. The study committee shall also prepare proposed articles of agreement that, if approved pursuant to the provisions of this chapter, shall serve as the operating agreement for the new union school district. At a minimum, articles of agreement shall state:
>
> (1) The name of any school district the study committee considers "necessary" to formation of the proposed union school district.
>
> (2) The name of any school district the study committee considers "advisable" to include in the proposed union school district.
>
> (3) The legal name or temporary legal name by which the union school district shall be known.
>
> (4) The grades, if any, that the proposed union school district will operate and the grades, if any, for which it will pay tuition.
>
> (5) The cost and general location of any proposed new school buildings to be constructed and the cost and general description of any proposed renovations to existing school buildings.
>
> (6) A plan for the first year of the union school district's operation for transportation of students, assignment of staff, and use of curriculum that is consistent with existing contracts, collective bargaining agreements, and other provisions of law. The board of the union school district, if formed, shall make all subsequent decisions regarding transportation, staff, and curriculum subject to existing contracts, collective bargaining agreements, and other provisions of law.
>
> (7) A list of the indebtedness of each "necessary" and "advisable" district, which the union school district shall assume.
>
> (8) The specific pieces of real property of each "necessary" and "advisable" district that the union school district shall acquire, their valuation, and how the union school district shall pay for them.
>
> (9) Consistent with the proportional representation requirements of the Equal Protection Clause of the U.S. Constitution, the method or methods of apportioning representation on the union school district board as set forth in subsections 711(d) (unified union school district), (e) (union elementary or union high school district), and (f) (weighted voting) of this chapter.
>
> (10) The term of office for each member initially elected to the union school district board, to be arranged so that one-third expire on the day of the second annual meeting of the union school district, one-third on the day of the third annual meeting, and one-third on the day of the fourth annual meeting, or as near to that proportion as possible.
>
> (11) The date on which the proposal to create the union school district and the election of initial union school district board members will be submitted to the voters.
>
> (12) The date on which the union school district will be solely responsible for the education of its resident students in the grades for which it is organized and will begin operating any schools, paying any tuition, and providing educational services.
>
> (13) Whether the election of board members, election of school district officers, votes on the union school district budget, or votes on other public questions, or any two or more of these, shall be by Australian ballot.
>
> (14) Any other matters that the study committee considers pertinent.

## Who helps the committee

The law created seven regional facilitators, hired by the Vermont Learning Collaborative, to run merger committee meetings across the state. Our region's facilitator is Craig Aarons-Martin. The facilitator runs the meetings and stays neutral on whether a merger is a good idea. The committee elects its own chair. Nicole Mace, from the Winooski School Board, is the chair.

The Agency of Education provides data and staff help when the committee asks.

## Good faith

The law says each district must take part "in good faith." The state's facilitators wrote a [Good Faith Participation Standard](/records/act-170-good-faith-participation-standard/) to explain what that means. In short: show up, share information, and let the study happen. Good faith does not require anyone to support a merger. Members may oppose a merger and vote no.

## What happens after the report

| When | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| By September 1, 2027 | The committee sends its final report. |
| Within 30 days of the report, and no later than December 1, 2027 | The Secretary of Education reviews any proposed merger and makes a recommendation to the State Board of Education. |
| By December 15, 2027 | The State Board of Education issues its findings on each proposed merger. |
| March 7, 2028 (Town Meeting Day) | If the committee proposed a merger, voters in each district named in the plan vote on it. |
| By July 1, 2029 | If voters approve, the new district takes over, starting with the 2029 to 2030 school year. |

## How a vote would work

If the committee recommends a merger, its plan names each district as either "necessary" or "advisable" to the new district.

- The new district forms only if voters in **every** "necessary" district vote yes.
- An "advisable" district joins only if its own voters vote yes.

That rule is in [16 V.S.A. § 713(b)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/16/011/00713). If the plan names all three of our districts as necessary, then all three must vote yes for a merger to happen.

## What the law does not do

- It does not merge any district by itself. Voters decide.
- It does not require the committee to recommend a merger.

If the committee finds a merger inadvisable, its members may vote to end the committee. Districts may then form other study committees under existing state law.

Section 13a lets a district that has not merged by July 1, 2028 propose joining a nearby union school district later. Its voters must approve, and the Legislature must then pass the proposal.

Section 13b says that a town may not leave a union school district through fiscal year 2035. If our districts merged, that rule would apply to the new district.

## Other parts of Act 170

Act 170 does more than set up merger committees. For example, it creates regional **Cooperative Educational Service Areas** (CESAs), which let districts share services such as purchasing and training. Burlington, South Burlington, and Winooski are all in the Chittenden Central CESA, along with Colchester and Essex Westford. The CESA is separate from this committee and does not decide on mergers. See [how this committee differs from the CESA](/cesa/).
