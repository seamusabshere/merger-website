This committee's official name is the **Chittenden Central CESA Merger Committee (Group 18)**. That leads some people to think this committee and the Chittenden Central CESA board are the same thing. They are not. Act 170 of 2026 created both, but they have different members and different jobs. The committee takes its name from the CESA's region.

In short: this committee is made of elected school board members, who answer to voters, studying a question that voters will decide. The CESA board governs a body built to share services. A district may send its superintendent, or someone the superintendent names, to the CESA board instead of an elected school board member.

## What the CESA is

A **Cooperative Educational Service Area** (CESA) is a group of school systems that share programs and services, so each one does not have to pay for its own. Act 170 divided Vermont into seven CESAs.

The Chittenden Central CESA has five districts: Burlington, Colchester, Essex Westford, South Burlington, and Winooski.

The CESA is permanent. It is a public body that can hire staff, sign contracts, and keep its own budget, paid for by fees from its districts and by grants. When a district asks and the CESA board agrees, the CESA must offer help with:

- special education;
- business, technology, and office services; and
- forming union school districts.

Starting July 1, 2027, it must also offer staff training, curriculum work, transportation, and planning for school buildings.

## What the CESA board is

Each district's school board names one person to the CESA board each year. That person may be a school board member or the superintendent, or someone the superintendent names. Each district has one vote. The board must meet at least four times a year. It held its first meeting on July 28, 2026.

## What this committee is

This committee exists to answer one question: would it be advisable for Burlington, South Burlington, and Winooski to form one school district? Its members are school board members from those three districts only. Burlington has 3 votes, South Burlington 2, and Winooski 1. See [Committee members](/members/).

The committee sends its report by September 1, 2027. If it recommends a merger, voters decide on March 7, 2028. If it finds a merger inadvisable, its members may vote to end the committee. See [Act 170 of 2026](/act-170/) for the full timeline.

## Why the names overlap

Act 170 used the seven CESA regions to organize the merger study. The law asked the Vermont Learning Collaborative to hire one facilitator for each CESA region. Each facilitator then sorted the districts in that region into merger committees. Our facilitator, Craig Aarons-Martin, covers the Chittenden Central region, so this committee carries the region's name.

The Vermont Learning Collaborative is itself a CESA, the one for southeastern Vermont. The facilitators work for it, not for the Chittenden Central CESA.

The Chittenden Central region has three merger committees, and they do not line up with the CESA:

- **Group 18:** Burlington, South Burlington, and Winooski. This committee.
- **Group 14:** Essex Westford, on its own.
- **Group 19:** Colchester, with Milton and the Grand Isle districts (Alburgh, Champlain Islands, and South Hero). Those four are in a different CESA, Champlain Valley North.

The law allows districts from different CESAs to study a merger together.

## Where the two meet

- The CESA board does not decide on mergers and does not run this committee. The law gives neither body any power over the other.
- The CESA must offer its districts help with forming a union school district, if a district asks and the CESA board agrees.
- A merger could change the CESA. By January 1, 2028, and again by December 1, 2028, the Agency of Education must suggest changes to CESA lines that take new merged districts into account. The Legislature decides whether to make them.
